# Word choices

This file gives the ASD-STE100 Issue 9 dictionary entries that this skill
uses: common words that are not approved and the approved words to use in
their place, approved words with a restricted meaning, approved words that
are approved in one part of speech only, approved words that writers often
replace without need, and some words that the dictionary does
not list. It is a summary in this skill's own words, not the dictionary
(Part 2 of the standard). The page of each row is the page of the entry in
the published standard. The official standard is the authority: it is free
on request from https://asd-ste100.org, and a user who has it should use it
where it and this file do not agree.

Each word in the standard's list of recurring errors (dictionary pages 145
and 146) is in this file: fit (v), follow (v), and test (v) are in the
"Words with a restricted approved meaning" table.

Approval is for one part of speech and one meaning (Rules 1.2 and 1.3). In
the dictionary, an approved word is in UPPERCASE and a word that is not
approved is in lowercase (dictionary introduction, p.132). In an
alternative, (TN) marks a technical noun and (TV) a technical verb (p.131).
Where an entry gives more than one alternative, select the alternative that
has the correct meaning for your sentence.

A word that is not in this file can still be approved or not approved. Do
not say that it is approved or not approved as a fact. Give your best
judgment, and tell the user that the word needs a check in the Issue 9
dictionary.

## Words with a restricted approved meaning

These words are approved, but only in the part of speech and meaning shown.
The meanings are short summaries. Writers often use them in a wider
general-English meaning, and the error is easy to miss because the word is
approved. When one of these words is in the text, compare its use with the
meaning given here.

| Approved word | Approved meaning (summary) | For other meanings or parts of speech, use | Page |
|---|---|---|---|
| ABOUT (prep) | Concerning a subject | APPROXIMATELY (adv), AROUND (prep) | 150 |
| ABOVE (prep) | At or to a higher physical position than an item | For limits: MORE THAN (Rule 9.2) | 150 |
| AIRBORNE (adj) | In flight, or used in flight | For dust or particles in the air: a different construction, for example "dust in the air" | 159 |
| ALL (adj) | The full quantity, before a noun ("all the bolts") | No other use. "All" in a different position (for example "Heat and loads can all cause wear") is not this use | 160 |
| BY (prep) | Only in mathematical operations ("divide by 2", "increase by 10 °C") | For an agent: the active voice (Rule 3.6). For a method or a means: a different construction, for example "When you ..." or WITH (prep) | 182 |

## Words that are not in the dictionary

These words are not in the Issue 9 dictionary. A word that is not in the
dictionary is not approved, unless it is a technical noun (Rule 1.5) or a
technical verb (Rule 1.12).

| Word | What to do |
|---|---|
| apparatus | Keep it only if it is a technical noun in the company glossary (Rules 1.5, 1.8). Without the glossary, say that it needs a glossary check. |
| back-feed | feed (v) is not approved (→ SUPPLY (v), p.249). Write the direction of the supply with approved words, unless the glossary lists the term as a technical verb (Rule 1.12, category 3 f). |
| contribute | It is not a technical verb in a general description. Use approved words, for example "can help to cause" (HELP (v), CAUSE (v)). |
| dose | Not a technical verb in general text. Use ADD (v) or a different approved verb that gives the action, with the quantity from the source. |
| explode | Rule 1.12 lists it as a technical verb for civil and military operations only (category 3 c). In other text, write CAUSE AN EXPLOSION (EXPLOSION (n), p.242). |
| handling | handle (v) is not approved (→ MOVE, TOUCH, USE, p.267), and the "-ing" noun is not a technical noun in general text (Rule 3.5). Use the verb: "when you touch the seal faces". |
| loss | lose (v) is not approved (→ DECREASE (v), p.304). Write "when the pressure decreases", not "a loss of pressure". |
| naked flame | A regional term (Rule 1.10). FLAME (n) is approved (p.251): "keep flames and sparks away from the battery". |
| series | In general text it is not a technical noun. Use a different construction, for example a vertical list (Rule 4.3). |
| squarely | Give the result that the source asks for with approved words, for example FLUSH (adj) or ALIGN (v), if the source shows which one. If it does not, keep the word and say that it needs a check. |
