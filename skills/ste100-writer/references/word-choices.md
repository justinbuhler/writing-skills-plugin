# Word choices

This file gives the ASD-STE100 Issue 9 dictionary entries that this skill
uses: common words that are not approved and the approved words to use in
their place, approved words with a restricted meaning, approved words that
writers often replace without need, and some words that the dictionary does
not list. It is a summary in this skill's own words, not the dictionary
(Part 2 of the standard). The page of each row is the page of the entry in
the published standard. The official standard is the authority: it is free
on request from https://asd-ste100.org, and a user who has it should use it
where it and this file do not agree.

Each word in the standard's list of recurring errors (dictionary pages 145
and 146) is in this file: fit (v), follow (v), and test (v) are in the
"Words with a restricted approved meaning" table.

Approval is for one part of speech and one meaning (Rules 1.2 and 1.3). In
the dictionary, an approved word is in UPPERCASE and a word that is not
approved is in lowercase (dictionary introduction, p.132). In an
alternative, (TN) marks a technical noun and (TV) a technical verb (p.131).
Where an entry gives more than one alternative, select the alternative that
has the correct meaning for your sentence.

A word that is not in this file can still be approved or not approved. Do
not say that it is approved or not approved as a fact. Give your best
judgment, and tell the user that the word needs a check in the Issue 9
dictionary.

## Words that are not in the dictionary

These words are not in the Issue 9 dictionary. A word that is not in the
dictionary is not approved, unless it is a technical noun (Rule 1.5) or a
technical verb (Rule 1.12).

| Word | What to do |
|---|---|
| apparatus | Keep it only if it is a technical noun in the company glossary (Rules 1.5, 1.8). Without the glossary, say that it needs a glossary check. |
| contribute | It is not a technical verb in a general description. Use approved words, for example "can help to cause" (HELP (v), CAUSE (v)). |
| series | In general text it is not a technical noun. Use a different construction, for example a vertical list (Rule 4.3). |
